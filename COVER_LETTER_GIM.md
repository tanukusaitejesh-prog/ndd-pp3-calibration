Dear Editors,

We submit our Article, "Computational and location evidence are not independent in missense variant classification: label-free and functional measurement, cost, and tests of rules", for consideration in *Genetics in Medicine*.

ACMG/AMP classification adds computational evidence (PP3), hotspot or domain evidence (PM1) and gene-level evidence (PP2) as though they were independent. ClinGen caps PP3 with PM1 only at Strong, UK guidance advises against counting constraint in both PM1 and PP2, and other specifications differ again. These positions rest on expert judgement, because the dependence had not been measured on truth that the codes themselves did not produce.

We measured it in two such ways, and found the following:

- **Label-free.** On de novo missense excess in 47,630 trios and probands, joint PP3 and PM1 evidence was 0.44 of the product of their separate evidence, replicated in two further cohorts.
- **Functional.** On functional assays with PM1 residues as curated by ClinGen expert panels, it was 0.59 across five genes, while random residues showed no dependence (1.00). This test was pre-registered.
- **Cost.** Stacked, the three criteria over-count by about two points, the width of a classification step. The overlap is not merely between genes: within known disease genes, where curators apply PM1, PP3 beside PM1 still over-counts 0.33–0.42 points at Supporting in every cohort (all intervals above zero).
- **Rules.** Trained in one de novo cohort and tested in two others under pre-registration, no rule bettered ClinGen's cap for PP3. The cap without PP2 beside PM1 reproduced the joint evidence of all three criteria within half a point, whereas adding PP2 over-counted by one to two points.
- **Practice.** Across 94,050 expert-panel and laboratory classifications, the cap bound in under 5% of PP3-with-PM1 combinations, and stronger PP3 beside PM1 has spread since calibrated strengths were introduced.

We also report a methodological result of wider use: on truth re-scored from curators' own classifications, negative-control criteria with no location content show the same apparent dependence. Such truth therefore cannot measure dependence without negative controls.

We think the paper suits *Genetics in Medicine* because it gives the measurement behind current ClinGen and UK rules, at a time when the next version of the classification standard is grouping related evidence. Its recommendations are concrete: keep the cap, do not add PP2 beside PM1, and test a further limit for Moderate PP3 prospectively.

The study used only public, de-identified data. Code, the analysis log, four pre-registrations committed before their analyses (with a deviation log), and all outputs are in a repository that will be public with a Zenodo DOI at submission. Pre-registered null and failed results are reported in the main text.

This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors approved the submission. [Competing interests statement.]
